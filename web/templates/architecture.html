2. Mathematical Formulations & Index Logic

A. Cosine Similarity via Dot Product

Cosine similarity measures the angle between two geometric vectors in $\mathbb{R}^D$:

$$\text{sim}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^D u_i v_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^D u_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^D v_i^2}}$$

Because our mock_embed() function normalizes each vector by dividing by its Euclidean norm, $\|\mathbf{u}\| = \|\mathbf{v}\| = 1$, reducing the calculation strictly to the dot product $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}$.

B. Empirical Recall@K Metric

Recall@K quantifies the fidelity of approximate methods against exact ground truth:

$$\text{Recall}@K = \frac{|\mathcal{N}_{\text{approx}}^{(K)}(\mathbf{q}) \cap \mathcal{N}_{\text{exact}}^{(K)}(\mathbf{q})|}{K}$$

A recall of $100\%$ means the approximate method retrieved identical top-k neighbors as brute-force search. Values $>90\%$ are typical production targets for LLM retrieval.

C. IVF (Inverted File Index) & Lloyd's K-Means

IVF partitions $N$ vectors into $n_{\text{list}}$ Voronoi cells with centroids $\{\mathbf{c}_1, \dots, \mathbf{c}_k\}$. At query time, only $n_{\text{probe}}$ clusters are inspected:

$$\mathcal{C}_{\text{probe}} = \underset{\mathcal{C} \subset \{1..n_{\text{list}}\}, |\mathcal{C}|=n_{\text{probe}}}{\operatorname{argmax}} \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} (\mathbf{c}_c \cdot \mathbf{q})$$

Vectors compared per query drops to $\approx \frac{n_{\text{probe}}}{n_{\text{list}}} \times N$. However, true neighbors landing outside probe cells are lost.

D. Navigable Small World (NSW / HNSW)

Vectors form vertices in a graph with high clustering and short path lengths. Greedy best-first routing explores edges maximizing similarity:

$$v_{t+1} = \underset{u \in \text{Neighbors}(v_t)}{\operatorname{argmax}} (\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{q})$$

The search terminates when no candidate in the frontier expands beyond the worst element in the priority queue bounded by ef_search.
